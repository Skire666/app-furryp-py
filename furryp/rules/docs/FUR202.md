## What it measures

The Halstead metric of a function, computed on its tokens: `volume` by
default, or `difficulty`, `effort` or `bugs` with the `metric` option. The
definitions are those of `halstead-file` (FUR201).

The tokens of a function go from the `def` or `async` keyword to its end:
the signature and nested functions are included, the decorators are not.
Every function is measured, methods included; a nested function counts in its
parent and is also evaluated alone.

The diagnostic points at the name of the function, written with its qualified
name (`Parser.load`, `outer.inner`).

## Why it matters

A function with a high volume does a lot, even in few lines.

## How to fix it well

- Extract a step into a well-named function.
- Look for values used everywhere: they often belong in a helper or in a small
  object.
- Remove duplication: replace similar statements with data and a loop.

## What not to do

- Shortening variable names: the length of a name is not counted.
- Reusing one variable for several purposes to reduce the vocabulary.
- Moving the same statements to a helper called only once, without a meaning
  of its own.

## Example

With `max` lowered to 70 to keep the example short:

```json
{"rules": {"halstead-function": {"max": 70}}}
```

Flagged (volume 123.2; `total` is repeated 4 times):

```python
def invoice_total(lines, discount):
    total = sum(line.price for line in lines)
    total = total - discount
    return max(total, 0)
```

Fixed (each function does one thing: volumes 53.8 and 60.9):

```python
def subtotal(lines):
    return sum(line.price for line in lines)


def invoice_total(lines, discount):
    return max(subtotal(lines) - discount, 0)
```
